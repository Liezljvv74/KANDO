# Kando: foundation design for the rebuilt Skilled marketplace

26 October 2026. Status: for owner approval, then a Stage 1 plan. This document is the handover from the current repository (skill-web, Payload CMS + Next.js + Expo) to a new repository, **Kando**, under the same GitHub account. It describes what the first release must do, the decisions already made, and what is left to the builder. The owner reviews and tests; they do not write code. Every change goes through a pull request that the owner merges. After every stage a test report is written in `Reports/`.

Naming: Kando is the repository name. The product is called **SkillWeb** for now (owner decision, 26 October 2026; it may be renamed KANDO later, so the name is a single setting in the text catalogue and the app config, never hard-coded in screens). The domain stays skillweb.space.

---

## 1. Why rebuild, and why now

Skilled is a service marketplace for Germany: households book trusted, verified local helpers and small businesses, and providers get paid without lead fees. The current code was built on Payload CMS. Payload is a content system; Skilled is a transaction system. The project has already had to work around Payload's access model (a request wrapper plus row-level security so that user data stays safe), has no realtime for chat and no job queue for payments, and keeps two copies of every user-facing text. The next stages (bookings, payments, invoices, disputes, chat) do not fit a content system.

Nobody uses the app yet, and production holds only test data. That makes a clean rebuild cheaper than an incremental one: one new repository, one cutover, no period with two backends.

The research of 26 October 2026 (`Marketing/`, git-ignored) fixed the value proposition the rebuild serves:

> Skilled is where people in Germany book and pay verified local helpers and small businesses legally: the money is held until the job is done, the invoice writes itself, and the provider pays nothing until they have been paid.

Four pillars: **paid when it's done** (customer), **no fee without a job, one public rate** (provider), **paperwork included** (both), **verified providers, clear rules** (both). Owner decisions that bind this design: payout to the provider within 7 days of confirmation; a person decides disputes within 14 working days; no promise of support staff, hotlines or response times until staffing is funded; the commission percentage is still open (tested at 10, 12 and 15 percent in the validation interviews).

## 2. The first release

**The owner's test for launch day:** a customer finds a verified provider, books, pays, and both leave ratings. Both booking styles must work, and customer and provider can message each other in the app.

Everything below is the scope of release 1. It is bigger than "parity with today"; parity is the first stage inside it.

### 2.1 Goals

1. **Find.** Search by category and postcode (German postcodes, distance in km, remote providers included), provider pages with photo, services, prices, skills, references, ratings and the verified mark. Public provider pages on the website for search engines; the marketplace itself runs in the phone app.
2. **Book, two ways.** Direct: a service with a price (fixed, hourly or "from") is booked for a date and time. Request: the customer describes a job (text, photos, category, postcode, preferred dates), matching providers send quotes (price, date, note), the customer accepts one and that becomes the booking. Requests expire after a deadline.
3. **Pay when it's done.** The customer enters a payment method at booking; nothing is charged. The customer confirms the job in the app, or it counts as done 7 days after the appointment if nothing was reported; the agreed amount is then charged and the provider is paid out within 7 days, minus Skilled's one commission. A declined charge gets retries and reminders. Disputes freeze the charge; a staff decision within 14 working days ends in full, partial or no charge.
4. **Paperwork included.** Each completed job produces an invoice or receipt in the provider's name, generated by Skilled, with the wording for the provider's status (private individual, Kleinunternehmer, business with VAT), labour and material split so the customer can claim section 35a EStG, and a yearly export. Skilled sends each provider a monthly invoice for its commission. Tax identification data is collected for DAC7 and explained in plain language.
5. **Trust.** A provider is verified when Stripe Connect payout onboarding is complete (Stripe checks identity). Categories that need a document (babysitting: extended police certificate) keep staff review; a service is hidden until its documents are approved. Two-way ratings after paid jobs only. Cancellation rules for both sides. No-show rule: the first provider no-show in twelve months triggers a warning in the app; the second triggers a 30-day suspension with the reason shown, an appeal, and payout of earnings already due.
6. **Talk.** In-app messaging between customer and provider inside a request or booking thread, text and photos, delivered in realtime, kept as evidence for disputes.
7. **Translate.** The translate button from the current repository is carried over: provider texts translated into the viewer's language on demand, labelled as AI-translated, stored and reused.
8. **Run it.** A staff back office with two roles, Administrator and Reviewer: document review; look up providers and customers, suspend with a stated reason, handle appeals; bookings and their money state, dispute decisions, refunds, payouts; hide a rating; categories and required documents; pages (Impressum, Datenschutz, AGB, Ranking, any other page, a blog later) in German and English; staff accounts and roles. More screens are added as needed.
9. **Two languages.** German default, English throughout, in one text catalogue shared by app, website and back office.

### 2.2 Non-goals for release 1

- Apple and Google sign-in (email and password only at launch; social sign-in later).
- A separate automated ID check beyond Stripe's; revisit later.
- Deposits, partial payments, SEPA direct debit, PayPal, cash recording.
- South Africa or any second market (but see section 5: nothing may make it hard).
- Languages other than German and English as app languages.
- Meisterpflicht trades, providers based outside Germany, recurring bookings.
- A web version of the marketplace; the website shows public pages and sends people to the app.
- Translating customer-written content (requests, chat, ratings).

### 2.3 Anti-goals (a failure even if it works)

- Lead fees, pay-to-message, subscriptions, paid placements, ads, a social feed.
- Promising human support, response times, "Treuhand", "Escrow" or "Garantie" anywhere in product or marketing text.
- Any endpoint that lets a client read or change another user's data; any table without a row-level security policy.
- Prices or hours set by the platform for providers, or directing their work (Platform Work Directive).
- Machine-translated or AI-generated text shown without a label.
- A second copy of any user-facing text.
- Competitor names in code, docs, commits or GitHub.

## 3. Users

- **Customers:** private persons and businesses anywhere in Germany, in the app, with an account (needed to book; browsing is open).
- **Providers:** based in Germany; private individuals, side businesses (Kleinunternehmer) and businesses. One account type: a person can be customer, provider or both.
- **Staff:** the owner as Administrator; Reviewers later.

Launch categories (from the current seed): photography, DJ, catering, digital services, marketing, management and consulting, dog walking, babysitting (requires the extended police certificate), cleaning, moving and clearance, assembly, gardening, small repairs, other services. Marketing starts with household and personal services in two or three cities; the product is Germany-wide.

## 4. Decided: how it gets made

| Part | Decision | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Repository | New GitHub repository `Kando`, same account; one monorepo | Clean cutover; one place for app, web, admin, API |
| Phone app | Expo (React Native), Expo Router, EAS builds, package `space.skillweb.app` kept | Native app, store distribution, the existing dev build keeps working |
| Website, back office, API | Next.js (App Router) on Vercel, Frankfurt region | Owner knows Next.js; one deployment; server rendering for public pages |
| Back office UI | Refine inside the Next.js app, under `/admin` | Staff screens in hours per resource; no CMS owning the schema |
| Database | Neon Postgres, Frankfurt, PostGIS, row-level security; new branch or database for the new schema | Already in use; geo search; dev branching |
| Schema and migrations | Drizzle, in `packages/db`, designed fresh (not pulled from Payload) | The project owns its schema; country, currency and ledger from day one |
| Auth | Neon Auth, email and password with email confirmation and password reset; Apple and Google later | Store-compliant without extra work at launch |
| Files | Neon Object Storage (photos public bucket, documents private bucket), Frankfurt | Already in use |
| Realtime | Neon Realtime for chat delivery (fallback: polling) | Same vendor; chat needs it |
| Background jobs | Inngest (free tier): payment capture and retries, payouts, invoice PDFs, emails, request expiry, auto-confirmation, suspension expiry | Serverless functions cannot hold long work |
| Payments | Stripe: Connect Express for providers, saved payment method at booking, charge on confirmation, behind a `payments` interface | Germany first; a second gateway later behind the same interface |
| Email | Resend (EU) | Already in use |
| Shared contract | `packages/contract`: Zod schemas and TypeScript types for every request and response, used by API, admin and app | One definition; a server change breaks the app at compile time |
| Texts | `packages/i18n`: one catalogue (German, English) used by app, website, admin and emails; texts stay in code and go through review | No duplicated texts |
| Testing | Local Postgres in Docker for integration tests; GitHub Actions on every pull request (type check, lint, tests); EAS build on release branches | Fast, trustworthy green |
| Deployment | Vercel preview per pull request; production on merge; `pnpm migrate` run by the owner's instruction only | Same discipline as today |
| Documents | Invoice and receipt PDFs generated in a job, stored in the private bucket, linked from the booking | Paperwork pillar |

### 4.1 Repository layout

```
apps/mobile      Expo app
apps/web         Next.js: website, /admin (Refine), /api (route handlers), Inngest functions
packages/db      Drizzle schema, migrations, RLS statements, seeds
packages/contract Zod schemas and types shared by apps
packages/i18n    text catalogue (de, en)
packages/domain  booking, payments, invoices, disputes, ratings, translation: plain modules used by apps/web
Reports/         test reports per stage
docs/            this design, stage plans, decisions
```

Route handlers in `apps/web` only parse input against the contract, check the caller, and call `packages/domain`. Nothing in `packages/domain` imports Next.js.

### 4.2 Data model, the shape (names are the builder's)

- `users` (one row per person; email, password hash via Neon Auth, language, roles customer/provider/staff, staff role admin/reviewer, suspension state with reason and until-date).
- `providers` (profile: display name, public name, bio, strengths, skills, experience, base postcode, location point, service radius, works remotely, status draft/live/suspended, legal status private/kleinunternehmer/business, VAT id, tax id for DAC7, Impressum, Stripe account id, verified-at).
- `services` (provider, category, title, description, price type, amount, **currency**), `references`, `photos`, `documents` (requirement code, file, status, reviewer, note).
- `categories` (slug, names per language, required document codes, **country**), `postcodes` (**country**, code, place, state, location; unique on country and code; per-country format), `countries`, `currencies`.
- `requests` (customer, category, text, photos, postcode, preferred dates, deadline, status), `quotes` (request, provider, amount, date, note, status).
- `bookings` (customer, provider, service or quote, date and time, amount, currency, status: requested, confirmed, done, disputed, cancelled, charged, paid out; confirmation deadline; cancellation record; no-show record).
- `payments`: `payment_methods` (Stripe references only), `charges`, `payouts`, and a double-entry `ledger` (every money movement, never updated).
- `invoices` (booking, number, issuer = provider, recipient, lines with labour and material, VAT wording, PDF file), `commission_invoices` (provider, month, PDF).
- `disputes` (booking, opened by, reason, messages, decision, decided by, decided at).
- `ratings` (booking, from, to, stars, text, hidden flag).
- `threads` and `messages` (request or booking, sender, text, photo, read markers).
- `warnings` and `suspensions` (user, reason code, until, lifted by).
- `translations` (hash, language, text), `translation_requests` (hashed client key, created at).
- `pages` and `posts` (slug, per-language title and body, published).

Every table with user data has a row-level security policy; public reference data (categories, postcodes, pages) is readable by all. Money rows are append-only.

### 4.3 Rules that are part of the design

- Payment method required at booking; charge on confirmation; auto-confirmation 7 days after the appointment if nothing was reported; payout within 7 days of confirmation; dispute decision within 14 working days; outcomes full, partial or no charge.
- Cancellation (my call, strike if wrong): free up to 24 hours before the appointment for both sides; later cancellations and no-shows are recorded and shown in ratings; the customer is never charged for a provider no-show.
- No-show rule as in goal 5; 48 hours for the provider to dispute a reported no-show before it counts (my call).
- Request deadline: the customer picks 1 to 7 days, default 3 (my call); quotes expire with the request.
- Ratings: both sides, 1 to 5 stars and text, only after a charged booking, visible once both have rated or after 14 days (my call); staff can hide.
- Verified mark: Stripe Connect onboarding complete and payouts enabled.
- Final amount for hourly and "from" prices: the booking records an estimate and a cap of 20 percent above it; at completion the provider enters the final amount (hours, or price plus material lines); the customer approves it as part of confirming; above the cap the customer must approve explicitly in the app before the charge.
- Direct bookings propose a date and time; the provider accepts or declines within 24 hours, otherwise the booking lapses. No calendar or time slots in release 1.
- Job address: entered by the customer at booking or with a request, stored privately with its own access rule, shown to the provider only once the booking is accepted; it is the invoice recipient address.
- Failed charges: Skilled retries and reminds; the customer's account is blocked from new bookings until paid; the provider is paid out only after the charge has succeeded. Skilled never fronts money it has not received. Chargebacks after payout are deducted from the provider's next payout only when the dispute decision went against the provider; otherwise Skilled absorbs them.
- Commission: one percentage on the total amount the customer pays; card processing fees are inside the commission; no customer-side fee.
- Disputes: a customer can open a dispute until the charge (within the 7-day window, which freezes the charge); after the charge a 14-day complaint window can end in a refund, deducted from the provider's pending payout or the next one. Reminders before auto-confirmation say plainly that the charge is coming.
- Widerruf: the booking flow records consent to early performance and shows the Button-Lösung wording; the 14-day right is explained in the terms.
- Legal pages by slug per language: `/impressum`, `/datenschutz`, `/agb`, `/ranking`, with a ranking explanation (P2B).
- Data residency: everything in Frankfurt (Neon, Vercel, Object Storage, Resend EU); OpenRouter and the model provider named in the privacy notice and processor list.
- Texts: every user-facing text exists in German and English; AI output is labelled.

## 5. Country-ready rules (South Africa later)

South Africa follows as a second market after the German launch (owner decision, 26 October 2026; a South African company will be set up). Nothing is built for it now. These rules keep it cheap later:

- Postcodes keyed by country and code with a per-country format check; a country on providers, customers (when addresses arrive), categories and bookings.
- Every amount stored with a currency; formatting in one function per surface; no "EUR" in labels.
- The payments module behind one interface; no Stripe objects in domain tables beyond opaque references.
- Languages open-ended: no two-language assumptions in types or switches; English must work as a first language.
- Verification requirement codes neutral (`police-certificate`, not a German term) and configurable per category and country.
- Legal texts, time zone and locale per market; no hard-coded "Frankfurt" or "DSGVO" in shared components outside the German texts.
- Any new Germany-only assumption is written into the stage plan as known debt.

## 6. Stages of release 1

Each stage gets its own plan, is built through pull requests the owner merges, and ends with a test report.

1. **Foundation and parity.** Monorepo, CI, Docker test database, Drizzle schema with RLS, Neon Auth, the shared contract and text catalogue, seeds (categories, postcodes, test users), the API for today's features, the Expo app moved over and pointed at the new API (sign-up, login, profiles, services, references, photos, documents, search, provider screen, translate button), the website's public pages, the back office with document review, user lookup and suspension, categories, pages, staff roles. Payload is not part of it. Done when every current feature works on the new foundation and the old repository can be archived.
2. **Booking and money.** Direct booking, requests and quotes, saved payment method, confirmation and auto-confirmation, charge, Stripe Connect onboarding and the verified mark, payouts, ledger, cancellations, no-show rule, disputes with staff decisions and refunds, invoices and receipts, commission invoices, DAC7 data collection, the Preise page with the fixed commission.
3. **Messaging and ratings.** Threads and messages with photos in realtime, read markers and push notifications, two-way ratings, rating moderation.
4. **Launch readiness.** Legal pages, privacy notice and processor list, ranking explanation, data export and deletion, placeholder graphics replaced, store listings, production migration, cutover of the Vercel project and the domain, archive of the old repository.

## 7. Constraints

- Owner merges every pull request; nothing is merged by the builder. A test report after every stage and after big changes.
- No AI co-author or "Generated with" lines in commits, pull requests or files. No competitor names anywhere in the repository.
- Every text users see exists in German and English.
- Security rule carries over in spirit: user data behind row-level security, every API route checks the caller, every new private table has a policy and an isolation test.
- Costs: free tiers during development; at launch Vercel Pro, Neon as needed, the Apple Developer account, Stripe and OpenRouter per use.

## 8. Left to the builder

- Table and column names, index design, the exact Drizzle and RLS setup, the migration tooling, the Inngest function layout.
- The Refine data provider and how roles map to screens; the exact admin screen set within goal 8.
- The request matching rule (which providers see a request: category, radius, remote).
- Chat delivery details, push notification service, offline behaviour within the Expo app.
- Invoice numbering scheme and PDF layout, within German invoice requirements (section 14 UStG, section 19 wording, labour and material split).
- The payments interface and the ledger entries per event.
- How the translate module is ported (same behaviour, Drizzle tables).
- Test data strategy for the Docker database and for Stripe test mode.

## 9. Decisions the owner still owes

- The commission percentage (after the validation interviews; the Preise page waits for it).
- The legal entity: it does not exist yet (26 October 2026). Commission invoices, the Impressum and the "SkillWeb issues the invoice on your behalf" consent are built with placeholders for legal name, address and tax status, and the entity becomes a launch-readiness item (Stage 4).
- The "my call" rules in section 4.3 (24-hour cancellation window, 48-hour no-show dispute window, request deadline default, rating visibility, 24-hour acceptance window, 20 percent cap) are accepted for now and may change later; each is a constant in one place.
- The product name (SkillWeb now, possibly KANDO later): one setting.
